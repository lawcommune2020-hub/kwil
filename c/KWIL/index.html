Investment highlights

What is working, with the document behind each point

Kwality Wall's (India) is the ice cream business that Hindustan Unilever demerged on 1 Dec 2025. It is now a standalone listed company with three of the world's five largest ice cream brands, a cold-chain network of more than 200,000 outlets, and a global parent that has just cut the royalty it charges. Its first year was a loss (as a start-up of a standalone company), and the last two quarters show the turn.

The case for

1
Growth has accelerated for two straight quarters, led by volume

Organic sales growth went from −6.5% (Q3 FY26) to +16.6% in Q1 FY27 and +42.2% in Q2 FY27, the best in 16 quarters. Volume did most of the work (+14.9% then +31.8%). Growth was broad: impulse (cones, sticks) at high double-digit volume growth, the relaunched in-home range at strong double digits, quick commerce at high double digits, and modern trade and general trade both up. The company notes favourable weather helped.

Margins are turning, with a gross margin of 46.4%

Gross margin was 41.5% in Q3 FY26 (hit by one-off trade investment and cocoa) and has risen to 45.6% in Q1 and 46.4% in Q2 FY27, even though the in-home range moved to a more expensive dairy recipe. The Magnum Ice Cream Company and the open offer

Who owns the company, what they paid, and the royalty arrangement
Royalty to the parent
0% → 1%

The royalty KWIL pays to Magnum IP Holdings B.V. is 0% until 31 Mar 2027, and 1% of turnover (net sales of licensed products) plus taxes in FY2027-28 and FY2028-29, under a three-year IP agreement to 31 Mar 2029 approved by the board on 29 Jun 2026.

The 0% also covers 1 Oct 2025 to 31 Mar 2026, so the royalty and central-service fees booked for that period were reversed: a ₹39.4 crore gain in Q1 FY27. The company describes the moratorium as support for its investments and stabilisation after the demerger. The agreement is a related-party transaction. One trade outlet reports the earlier rate as 5% of turnover; that figure is not in the filings we hold.

Magnum's calls and annual report on India

What the parent company has told its own investors about India

KWIL's parent, The Magnum Ice Cream Company (TMICC), is listed in Amsterdam, London and New York and reports separately. Its first annual report (for 2025) came out ahead of its 7 May 2026 annual meeting, and it has held results calls in Feb, Apr, Jul and Oct 2026 plus a conference talk in Sep. India has been part of its numbers since 1 April 2026, and it comes up on every call. All the documents are in the folder below. We paraphrase; open the transcripts for the exact words. These are the parent's statements, not KWIL's.

The parent's view of India
A big bet on India

TMICC's CEO, Peter ter Kulve, calls the Indian market like China in the early 1990s: very low consumption per person and a booming economy. He calls India 'the biggest growth opportunity in the industry' and says it would not surprise him if India became the world's largest ice cream market in 20 years, ahead of the US (call of 12 Feb 2026).

He is also candid that the business was a turnaround: it 'lost a lot of share' over 20 years and profit was flat in 2025. At a conference in Sep 2026 he called the India plan 'a big bet' that 'will increase our losses' first, and described what has been done: dairy instead of vegetable-fat recipes, prices cut about 30%, a new distribution model with about 70,000 cabinets placed a year, a new team, and five factories being built around India.

The business

Kwality Wall's (India) Limited (KWIL) is India's leading ice cream and frozen dessert company, operating the Kwality Wall's, Magnum, and Cornetto brands across 200,000+ retail outlets and 15,000+ push carts in 400+ cities. Demerged from HUL effective December 1, 2025 and listed February 16, 2026, the company is now 61.9% owned by The Magnum Ice Cream Company HoldCo 1 Netherlands B.V. (TMICC), the global ice cream spinoff from Unilever. Revenue is highly seasonal, concentrated in Q1 (April–June peak) and earned through its Impulse (Magnum, Cornetto) and In-home portfolio segments.

Our thesis

KWIL is a freshly-listed India pure-play on the world's most recognised ice cream brands, in a market with structurally low per capita consumption.

Q1 FY27 delivered the first profitable quarter (EBITDA ₹104.9 cr, margin 12.1%; OSG +16.6%) after two loss-making quarters post-demerger, demonstrating the In-home relaunch and Quick Commerce scale-up are gaining traction. What the market may not have fully priced is that full-year FY27 is almost certainly still loss-making at the PAT level due to deep off-season losses, while the stock trades 59% above what TMICC itself paid for the controlling stake (₹22.47/share implied vs ₹35.74 today). The primary risk is that the investment phase — elevated standalone overheads, capex of ₹213.9 cr on negative operating cash flow — extends longer than expected, and the 51× run-rate P/E is calculated off a peak-season quarter that flatters the full-year economics.

The case for

R1World-class brand portfolio with proven premiumisation: Magnum and Cornetto delivered strong volume growth in Q3 FY26 even when the overall business contracted, and OVG hit +14.9% in Q1 FY27. India's per capita ice cream consumption is a fraction of peer economies — a structural growth backdrop.▸ Exchange filing, 7 Mar 2026
“our power brands, Magnum and Cornetto, delivered strong volume growth, underscoring the resilience of our portfolio and continued consumer demand.”
R2Quick Commerce is a step-change channel that didn't exist in the old HUL reporting structure. It's delivering double-digit growth and expanding consumption occasions, particularly for premium SKUs, structurally raising the addressable revenue pool.▸ Exchange filing, 14 Aug 2026
“Quick Commerce remained a strong growth engine, delivering double-digit growth, while investments made in Modern Trade drove healthy growth”
R3Gross margin expanded to 45.6% in Q1 FY27 despite input cost headwinds, driven by mix (premiumisation) and disciplined pricing. This is above the 41.5% seen in Q3 FY26 even excluding the one-off trade provisions, confirming the In-home dairy relaunch and mix upgrade are working.▸ Exchange filing, 14 Aug 2026
“Gross margin improved to 45.6%, supported by a favourable product mix and disciplined pricing, despite higher input costs associated with the In-home portfolio transition to dairy.”
R4Parent TMICC has directly placed TMICC's CFO as Non-Executive Chairperson and its METSA President as Director. These are not passive board seats — both have track records in executing ice cream category scale-ups across emerging markets.▸ Exchange filing, 31 Mar 2026
“Mr. Abhijit Bhattacharya is the Chief Financial Officer of The Magnum Ice Cream Company N.V. having previously served as Chief Financial Officer for Unilever Ice Cream since 2024.”
R5Balance sheet is lean (debt ₹50.3 cr, D/E 0.07×) after the demerger, giving ample headroom for the ₹213.9 cr capex programme. The investment cycle appears front-loaded (FY26 capex for FY27+ season), reducing the risk of dilutive equity raises.▸ Price and financial data
“Balance sheet: debt/equity 0.07 · net cash -0.4% of market cap · operating cash flow –× profit”
R6Operating leverage is embedded once the standalone cost base stabilises. Employee and other operating expenses are elevated because KWIL is building its own infrastructure for the first time. As volumes scale on a largely fixed cost base, EBITDA margins in peak quarters should step up materially.▸ Exchange filing, 14 Aug 2026
“investments in capabilities and strategic growth priorities may keep the cost base elevated in the near term, these investments are expected to accelerate growth, drive scale, and deliver operating leverage as the business expands.”
